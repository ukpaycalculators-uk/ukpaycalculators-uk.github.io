NHS pay bands 2026/27: salary and take-home pay for every band

Agenda for Change pay in England, Scotland and Wales for 2026/27, and what each pay point comes to after income tax, National Insurance and NHS Pension contributions. Worked out with the same engine as the NHS take-home pay calculator.

Pay scales from 1 April 2026. Tax year 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027. Last updated 8 November 2026.

How the figures are worked out

  • Full-time hours: 37.5 a week in England and Wales, 36 in Scotland.
  • Basic pay only, with no unsocial hours, overtime, on-call or high cost area supplement.
  • Tax code 1257L, National Insurance category A and no student loan.
  • England and Wales on the UK income tax bands, Scotland on the Scottish bands, as set out on the method page.
  • A member of the NHS Pension Scheme, paying the contribution tier for that pay. Contributions come off before income tax, so they lower the tax bill but not National Insurance.

Monthly take-home is the yearly figure divided by 12. The hourly rate is the one the pay circulars use: salary divided by the full-time weekly hours times 52.143. A payslip with arrears, overtime or a different tax code will not match exactly.

England: a 3.3% rise from April 2026

The 2026/27 award is 3.3% on every pay point, announced on 12 February 2026 and paid from April. Band 2 has one pay point, Bands 3 and 4 have two, and Bands 5 to 9 have three. Staff normally reach the top of Band 3 after 2 years, Band 4 after 3, Band 5 after 4 and Bands 6 to 9 after 5. Each band links to its page on the main site, which adds part-time hours and the full pay journey.

Scotland: 3.75% on a 36-hour week

NHS Scotland pays more at every band, for a shorter full-time week. 2026/27 is the second year of a two-year deal: 3.75% on the 2025/26 rates, which were revised up when that year's inflation guarantee was triggered, taking the 2025/26 settlement to 4.4%. A further inflation guarantee payment may follow in March 2027. Bands 8a to 9 have two pay points, five years apart.

The gap in take-home is smaller than the gap in pay. Scottish income tax charges 21% on pay above £29,526 and 42% above £43,662 (after pension contributions), against 20% and 40% in the rest of the UK, and NHS Scotland's pension has nine contribution tiers, from 5.7% to 12.7%. At the top of Band 5, Scotland pays £3,996 a year more than England, but take-home is £2,510.55 a year more. The NHS Scotland pay calculator uses the Scottish scale, tax bands and pension tiers.

Wales: 3.3%, with a Living Wage floor

Welsh pay points rose by 3.3% on the Welsh 2025/26 scales, so they stay slightly above England's on most bands: Band 5 starts at £32,557 against £32,073. Bands 1 and 2 are held at the Living Wage Foundation rate of £26,300, and the Band 3 entry point was corrected to £26,813 on 1 June 2026. Full-time is 37.5 hours, and Welsh taxpayers pay the same income tax rates as England.

Why a pay step can add less than expected

NHS Pension contributions rise in tiers as pay goes up, and the new rate applies to the whole of the pay, not just the part above the boundary. A step that crosses a tier boundary therefore takes more from every pound. In England:

  • Band 5, intermediate to top: £4,451 more pay, with the rate going from 8.3% to 9.8%. Take-home rises by £2,440.66 a year, about 55% of the rise.
  • Band 8b, entry to intermediate: £4,314 more pay, from 10.7% to 12.5%. Take-home rises by £1,459.48, about 34%, because the step also sits in the 40% tax band.
  • Band 5, entry to intermediate: both points are in the 8.3% tier, so £2,519 more pay keeps £1,646.42, about 65%.

The payslip does not show the other side. In the scheme's 2015 section, pension builds up each year at 1/54th of pensionable pay, whatever the tier, so the £4,451 rise at the top of Band 5 adds £82.43 a year to the pension earned in that year. The tier only sets what the member pays towards it. The NHS pension calculator projects the pension from pay and years in the scheme.

Band 9 and the £100,000 line

The personal allowance is cut by £1 for every £2 of income over £100,000, which makes the effective income tax rate 60% between £100,000 and £125,140. NHS Pension contributions are taken before tax, so they count: what matters is pay after the contribution.

  • England, Band 9 entry (£112,782): the 12.5% contribution of £14,097.75 leaves £98,684.25, under £100,000, so the full allowance is kept.
  • Wales, Band 9 entry (£114,475): £100,165.62 after the contribution, £165.62 over the line, so £82 of allowance is lost.
  • England, Band 9 top (£129,783): £113,560.12 after the contribution, inside the taper.
  • Scotland, Band 9 entry (£127,521): £111,325.83 after the 12.7% contribution, also inside the taper, where the Scottish 45% advanced rate makes the effective rate 67.5%.

London weighting: the high cost area supplement

In England, staff in and around London are paid a high cost area supplement on top of basic pay: 20% in inner London (at least £5,794 and at most £8,746 a year), 15% in outer London (£4,870 to £6,137) and 5% in the fringe (£1,346 to £2,270).

At the Band 5 entry point, inner London adds £6,415 and takes monthly take-home from £2,040.21 to £2,351.13. Outer London adds the £4,870 minimum, for £2,268.52 a month. The supplement counts as pensionable pay, so in inner London Band 5 entry moves up to the 9.8% pension tier.

Part-time staff and student loans

  • Part-time: pay is pro rata to hours. Band 5 entry at 22.5 hours a week in England is £19,243.80 a year, about £1,364.54 a month after deductions. The pension tier goes by actual pay, so the rate drops to 6.5%.
  • Plan 2 student loan: 9% of pay above £29,385. At Band 5 entry in England that is £241.92 a year, taking monthly take-home to £2,020.05. Repayments are worked out on pay before pension contributions.
