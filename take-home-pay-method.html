How UK take-home pay is calculated in 2026/27

The steps, in the order they are applied, with the 2026/27 figure for every rate and threshold. This is the method the UK salary and take-home pay calculator uses.

Tax year 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027. Last updated 7 November 2026.

The inputs

Annual gross pay; where the person pays income tax (Scotland, or the rest of the UK); their tax code; any student loan plans; any pension contribution and how it is taken; and whether they are over State Pension age. Everything below is worked out on yearly figures, then divided into months and weeks at the end.

Step 1: the pay that tax is charged on

Salary sacrifice comes off first. It reduces the salary itself, so income tax, National Insurance and student loan repayments are all worked out on the lower figure.

A net pay pension contribution comes off next, but only for income tax. National Insurance and student loans are still charged on the pay before it.

A relief at source contribution does not change any of these figures. It is paid from take-home pay at 80% of its gross value, and the pension provider claims the other 20% from HMRC.

Step 2: the personal allowance

The standard personal allowance is £12,570, which is what the tax code 1257L means. It is reduced by £1 for every £2 of adjusted net income over £100,000, so it reaches zero at £125,140. Adjusted net income is total taxable income less certain reliefs, including pension contributions (grossed up for relief at source) and Gift Aid.

Between £100,000 and £125,140 each extra £1 of pay also removes 50p of allowance. That produces a marginal rate of 62% in England, Wales and Northern Ireland (40% tax, 20% from lost allowance, 2% NI) and 69.5% for a Scottish taxpayer (45% plus 22.5% plus 2%).

Step 3: income tax

Taxable income is the pay from step 1 less the allowance from step 2. It is then cut into bands. The bands are set on taxable income; the salary column shows where each one starts for someone with the full £12,570 allowance.

Step 4: National Insurance

Employees pay Class 1 National Insurance at 8% on earnings between the primary threshold of £12,570 and the upper earnings limit of £50,270, and 2% on everything above. The rates are the same in every part of the UK.

Payroll applies National Insurance to each pay period on its own, using £1,048 and £4,189 a month or £242 and £967 a week, rather than adding the year up. So a month with a bonus can cost more NI than the same pay spread evenly. The yearly figures here assume equal pay every period.

Employees stop paying National Insurance once they reach State Pension age, even if they keep working. Income tax carries on as before.

Repayments are worked out on the same pay as National Insurance: after salary sacrifice, before a net pay pension contribution. A Postgraduate Loan is repaid on top of any undergraduate plan, so someone with both pays 9% and 6% at the same time on pay above both thresholds.

Step 6: take-home pay and rounding

Take-home pay is gross pay less income tax, National Insurance, student loan repayments and whatever pension contribution leaves pay. Each line is rounded to the penny once, and the total is built from the rounded lines so that the parts always add up. Monthly pay is the yearly figure divided by 12, weekly by 52, and hourly is the weekly figure divided by the hours worked (37.5 unless stated).

Worked examples

£35,000 in England

  • Taxable income: £35,000 less £12,570 = £22,430
  • Income tax: 20% of £22,430 = £4,486.00
  • National Insurance: 8% of (£35,000 less £12,570) = £1,794.40
  • Take-home: £28,719.60 a year, £2,393.30 a month

£50,000 in Scotland

  • Taxable income: £37,430
  • Income tax: £3,967 at 19% (£753.73) + £12,989 at 20% (£2,597.80) + £14,136 at 21% (£2,968.56) + £6,338 at 42% (£2,661.96) = £8,982.05
  • National Insurance: £2,994.40, the same as anywhere else in the UK
  • Take-home: £38,023.55 a year, £3,168.63 a month

£110,000 in England

  • Allowance: £110,000 is £10,000 over the limit, so £5,000 is lost, leaving £7,570
  • Taxable income: £102,430
  • Income tax: £37,700 at 20% (£7,540) + £64,730 at 40% (£25,892) = £33,432.00
  • National Insurance: 8% of £37,700 (£3,016) + 2% of £59,730 (£1,194.60) = £4,210.60
  • Take-home: £72,357.40 a year, £6,029.78 a month
